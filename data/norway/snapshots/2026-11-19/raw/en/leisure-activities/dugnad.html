Tips til undervisningen

De fleste deltakere vil kjenne til dugnad i en eller annen form. Dugnad i storfamilien eller i naboskapet er kanskje noe de har vært med på. Vil dugnad i f. eks. barnehagen eller i borettslaget oppleves like forpliktende og selvfølgelig? Hva skal til for at man skal føle seg forpliktet? Kan deltakelse i dugnad føre til at man knytter nye kontakter og får et større nettverk?

Snakk sammen

Selv om dugnad i utgangspunktet er frivillig, vil de fleste føle seg forpliktet til å delta. Det blir også forventet at en deltar.
Vil det snakkes om de som ikke deltar? På hvilken måte? Hva om det er de samme som ikke stiller opp hver gang? Snakk om konsekvenser ved ikke å delta i et slikt fellesskap (snakk, utenforskap, manglende tilhørighet i lokalmiljøet, manglende integrering osv.)

Diskuter om disse konsekvensene også gjelder for andre arenaer (f. eks. oppmøte på foreldremøter, følge barn på fritidsaktiviteter, sosiale tilstelninger på jobb osv.)

Dugnad

Dugnad is voluntary unpaid work. Dugnad work is a community effort. It is a common form of work in clubs and associations. In a housing cooperative, for example, the residents often take part in a dugnad in spring to tidy up after the winter. It is common in many kindergartens for the parents to get together for a dugnad once or several times a year. Then they might wash the kindergarten’s toys or fix up the outdoor areas.

Many children in Norway take part in organised leisure activities, and many of the activities are run by the parents as a dugnad. The parents could e.g. organise a jumble sale or a cultural event to raise money for the association.

Dugnad is an old custom in Norway. There is a similar tradition in most farming communities around the world.

In 2004, the word ‘dugnad’ was voted Norway’s national word.

Talk together

  • What are your thoughts on dugnad? Are you already familiar with the concept?
  • Have you ever been invited to take part in a dugnad in Norway?
  • Some clubs and associations practise an arrangement where you can pay a certain amount to not take part in a dugnad. What do you think about that?

It’s Monday. Two fathers are picking up their children from kindergarten. They are talking about the weekend’s dugnad at the kindergarten.

Henrik: Nice to see you again! It was amazing how much we got done! The new playhouse turned out great!

Arne: Yes. It’s so nice to work together like that too. But Truls’ parents never come to any dugnad.

Henrik: I’ve noticed that too. Although it’s voluntary, it still doesn’t look good.

Arne: Yes. I realise there can be times when it doesn’t suit, but every time...?

Henrik: Mm. And I’m sure that Truls will use the playhouse just as much as the other children.

  • Talk about the above situation. Why do Henrik and Arne react in the way they do?
  • What do you think the consequence may be of not taking part in dugnad?
