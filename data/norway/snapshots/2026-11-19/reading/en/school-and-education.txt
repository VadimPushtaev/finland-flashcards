Norway has long-standing traditions in the area of school and education. In the 18th century, the country's economic situation was very poor. Education was nonetheless considered to be so important that it was decided that all children were to go to school, and that it was to be paid for by the state.

The content of a school education has changed a lot over the 300 years since compulsory education was introduced in Norway. In the 18th century, its purpose was to teach pupils about Christianity. Practical skills such as reading, arithmetic, cooking, sewing and woodwork were included in the school curriculum later on.

Many subjects are taught today that aim to give children and young people a good general education. They learn factual knowledge and basic skills such as reading, writing and arithmetic.

Children use digital tools from a young age. Children learn to find good information and use the information to solve a variety of problems. The also learn to think independently and work together with others.
