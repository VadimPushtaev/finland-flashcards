Kindergartens
https://samfunnskunnskap.no/en/school-and-education/kindergartens.html

All the activities in a kindergarten are planned on the basis of the Kindergarten Act and other rules and plans decided by the authorities.

The Kindergarten Act sets out that kindergartens shall build on fundamental Christian and humanist principles and be based on human rights. These values are charity, forgiveness, equality and solidarity. These values are also evident in most religions and life stances. Kindergartens shall promote democracy and equality and combat all forms of discrimination.

Cooperation with parents

Parents receive information from the kindergarten about the goals of its work and the activities the children will take part in. They are invited to parents’ meetings and individual meetings. The parents’ meetings give parents the opportunity to get to know each other and talk about what it's like to have young children.

Parents who do not speak Norwegian have the right to have an interpreter present at parents’ meetings.

Payment

Running a kindergarten is expensive. The municipality pays a substantial part of the cost and parents pay the rest. The Storting has nonetheless established a maximum price for how much parents have to pay for a kindergarten spot.

Low-income families can apply for a reduced user charge or free 'core time'. If you have more than one child at the same kindergarten, you pay a lower price. This is called a 'sibling discount'.
