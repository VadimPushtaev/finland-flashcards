# Legal basis for the proposed selection

This is an analysis of repository snapshots, not a fresh verification of current
law or a claim that our collection is the official exam syllabus. The controlling
scope evidence below is separate from the three sources of learner prose.

## Controlling content provision

L2, section 25 a, first paragraph (PDF line breaks normalized):

> Medborgarskapsprovet testar grundläggande kunskaper om hur det finländska samhället fungerar samt om individens rättigheter och skyldigheter. Frågorna i flervalsprovet ska basera sig på det allmänt tillgängliga läromedlet för medborgarskapsprovet som är utarbetat utifrån läromedlet i den samhällsorientering som avses i 25 § i lagen om främjande av integration (681/2023). Frågorna i provet ska gälla central lagstiftning i Finland, grundläggande och mänskliga rättigheter, jämlikhet, jämställdhet mellan könen, Finlands historia och kultur samt annan central kunskap som ingår i läromedlet för medborgarskapsprovet.

The final clause does not establish that every orientation passage will be
tested. Conversely, the explicitly listed subjects do not exhaust the broader
society-knowledge requirement. Chapter relevance is an editorial inference from
this provision, supported where possible by the proposal's more concrete examples.

## Why the outline extends beyond a law-and-politics handbook

P1, PDF p. 10, explicitly identifies basic knowledge of housing, healthcare,
working life, taxation, education and study opportunities. It also mentions
illness, danger/emergencies and traffic culture. The same page supports geography,
holidays and customs. These are reasons to retain core national knowledge in
those domains, not to retain every personal-advice paragraph on those subjects.

P2, PDF p. 15, describes the orientation content as including integration goals
and services, employment and entrepreneurship, the labour market, study
opportunities, organizations/volunteering and participation, financial planning
and other everyday activities. Thus a topic cannot be discarded simply because
it is not named individually in section 25 a.

P1's distinction between general understanding and very detailed information
supports omitting form-filling, CV formatting, appointment booking and provider
directories. These are editorial exclusions, not legislative prohibitions on
particular questions.

## Selection rules applied

1. Keep a topic when its connection to an enacted subject or the supported wider
   civic scope can be explained. Keep the context needed to understand it.
2. Separate civic rights/duties and institutions from task-specific coaching.
   A procedure can still teach a relevant duty: emergency action, voting secrecy,
   legal remedies and compulsory registration must not be discarded merely
   because they describe actions.
3. Retain broad topic coverage, but select one main explanation for overlap.
   Source priority only breaks ties: orientation website, textbook, InfoFinland.
4. Prefer locally supported newer passages for contradicted facts. Do not
   silently edit an older passage or add factual text from the legal reports.
5. Use the legal/official archive for scope and conflict checks, not as a fourth
   source of learner prose. Any uncovered requirement remains a metadata gap.
6. Exclude exam format, registration, fees, score thresholds and exemption rules
   from learner chapters; they are not part of this collection's subject content.

The proposal outline is not yet a final paragraph selection. That follows the
first review and is demonstrated in the rights-and-obligations pilot.
